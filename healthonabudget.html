Health on a Budget


Taking care of your health does not have to mean spending a lot of money. Small, practical choices, such as preparing affordable, balanced meals, staying physically active, getting enough sleep, and making use of available healthcare services, can help you build healthier habits without stretching your budget. The key is knowing where to prioritize your spending, which costs you can avoid, and how to make everyday decisions that support your long-term well-being.

Mental Health

Looking after your mental health does not require expensive apps or activities. Simple habits and meaningful connections can support your wellbeing.

Stay Connected
Talk with friends, spend time with family, or check in on someone. Meaningful social connections can provide emotional support without costing anything.

Move More
Walking, stretching, dancing, and free home workouts are accessible ways to stay active and support your physical and mental wellbeing.

Manage Stress for Free
Try journaling, practice mindfulness, reading, or spending time in nature. Experiment with different activities to discover what helps you relax.

Build Healthy Habits
Establish consistent routines, set realistic goals, and celebrate small victories to build lasting healthy behaviors.

Know When to Seek Help
Self-care cannot replace professional treatment when it is needed. If symptoms persist, affect daily life, or become difficult to manage, consider seeking professional support. If cost is a concern, explore available community or healthcare services.

Remember, Taking care of your mental health means using the resources available to you and recognizing when you need additional support.

Fitness & Wellness

Get Moving Without Overspending, you do not need an expensive gym membership to stay fit. Walking, home workouts, and outdoor exercise can help you build strength and improve your health at little or no cost.

Make Everyday Movement Count
Walk short distances when practical, take the stairs when appropriate, and move regularly during long periods of sitting. The NHS recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week, alongside muscle-strengthening activities at least twice a week.

Do Not Underestimate Walking
Regular walking is associated with benefits for heart health, blood sugar regulation, mood, and overall fitness. Start with manageable walks and gradually increase your pace or duration.

Build a Sustainable Routine
Start slowly, focus on good technique, and increase the challenge as your fitness improves. A simple routine you can maintain is more useful than an expensive plan you rarely follow.

Research has also associated recreational running with lower mortality risk, although observational studies cannot prove that running alone causes these outcomes. You can still benefit from choosing activities appropriate for your ability. Remember, Effective fitness is about consistency, not how much money you spend.

Nutrition

Eating well does not require expensive health foods or supplements. Affordable everyday ingredients can provide the nutrients you need when you plan meals and reduce food waste.
Affordable HealthyFood Recommendations

  1. Protein:
    • Eggs - contains both protein and health fats
    • Tempe and tofu - Affordable plant-based protein.
    • Chicken - Protein for maintaining and repairing body tissues.
    • Beans and lentils - Protein, carbohydrates, and fibre.
    • Milk or plain yogurt - Protein and calcium.
    • Canned sardines or tuna - Convenient protein sources.
  2. Carbohydrates:
    • Rice - An affordable, versatile staple.
    • Potatoes and sweet potatoes - Carbohydrates and other nutrients.
    • Oats - Carbohydrates and fibre.
    • Corn and bananas - Convenient sources of carbohydrates.
  3. Fats:
    • Peanuts - Unsaturated fats, protein, and fibre.
    • Eggs and tempe - Provide both protein and fat.
    • Vegetable oils - Sources of dietary fat; use moderate amounts.
    • Seeds - Provide unsaturated fats, depending on the type.
  4. Fibre, Vitamins, and Minerals:
    • Cabbage, carrots, and leafy greens.
    • Seasonal fruits.
    • Frozen vegetables.
    • Beans and lentils.
The Art of a Simple Balanced Meal
The list above can be a guide in building a simple meal by combining different foods from each category. For example: rice, tempe, and cabbage provide a combination of carbohydrates, protein, fat, fibre, and micronutrients. Substitute ingredients based on your budget and availability.
Remember, A balanced diet does not depend on expensive products. Prioritize variety, affordable staples, and less food waste.

Health Essentials

It's important to know your body, simple self-checks can help you notice unusual changes early. They do not diagnose illness or replace recommended medical screening, but they can help you recognize when to seek professional advice.

  • Check your Skin,Look for new or changing moles, irregular borders, changes in colour or size, and sores that do not heal. Remember to check less visible areas, including your scalp, back, feet, and beneath your nails.
  • Notice Breast/Chest Changes, Pay attention to unusual lumps, changes in skin texture or shape, nipple changes, or unexpected discharge. Report new or unexplained changes to a healthcare professional.
  • Be Aware of Testicular Changes, If you have testicles, notice unusual lumps, swelling, pain, or changes in appearance. Seek medical advice if something seems different from normal.
  • Know When to Seek Help, Not every lump or change indicates cancer, but unusual symptoms deserve appropriate assessment. A healthcare professional can determine whether further testing is needed.
Remember, Self-checks help you notice changes; they are not a substitute for professional assessment or recommended screening.
