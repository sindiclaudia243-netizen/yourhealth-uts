Senior Citizen's Health


As we grow older, we may experience some massive changes in our living situations. The loss of loved ones. Declining mobility. Retirement. Living alone. Coping with degrading physical health. Such changes will surely affect our physical and emotional state. Aging is a natural part of life. And, every stage comes with its own changes and challenges. Taking care of yourself along the way can help you enjoy these years with better health. From looking after your mental well-being and staying active to choosing nutritious meals and getting regular health checkups, small steps can make a meaningful difference. The goal isn't just to live longer. It's to stay healthy, independent, and enjoy life to the fullest.

Mental Health

Daily Habits for Better Mental Well-being 🌱

To maintain a good mental health, you'll have to pay attention to your emotional, social, and physical well-being. Although some life changes are sadly unavoidable, we can still find ways to learn on how to adapt and ways to feel better. Small but consistent habits can make a difference in how you feel each day. You don't have to change everything at once, you can start with activities you enjoy and activities that are suitable for your needs 🤗

  • Move your body regularly

    • You can try yoga, aerobic, walking, gentle stretching, hiking, cycling, or other activities appropriate for your fitness level and health conditions.

  • Eat balanced meals and drink enough water

    • Make sure to stay hydrated and eat nutritious food. It supports your overall health. According to a review study, a healthy diet for older adults consists of plenty of plant-based foods, adequate protein-rich foods, and healthy fats. For example, spinach and broccoli; bananas and oranges; eggs and chicken; and avocados and nuts.

  • Regular sleep schedule

    • Maintaining a regular sleep helps in synchronizing your body's internal clock. Irregular sleep schedules can cause mood fluctuations. Consistent sleeping and waking times, however, would create a comfortable bedtime routine.

  • Make time for enjoyable activitiesy

    • You can listen to your favorite music, read your favorite book, cook your favorite dish, pray or reflect if it's meaningful to you, or pursue a favorite hobby to make life more meaningful

  • Staying socially connected

    • Keep in touch with your acquaintances to stay socially connected. A simple message or phone call can be a starting point - you never know! Life changes can sometimes be difficult to deal with, especially when you have a lot on your plate. If you're feeling overwhelmed, try talking to someone you trust. A friend or family member perhaps. Sharing your feelings can help you cope with the changes that might feel too overwhelming for you. You don't have to handle everything on your own :)

  • Practice mindfulness and relaxation

    • Deep breathing or quiet time can help you manage everyday stress. You also can write your thoughts and feelings in a journal

  • Limiting habits that may affect well-being

    • Please don't rely on alcohol to cope with difficult feelings… and try to discuss concerns about medicines or sleep with a healthcare professional.

  • Stay engaged in life and be positive

    • Retirement or having your children move out of the family home might bring big changes to your daily life. You might feel lonely, or unsure of what to do with your extra time. During these moments, having something to look forward to can help you feel more motivated!! Try spending time on activities to stay engaged in life. You can do your hobby or reconnect with old friends. Find enjoyment from your daily life

  • Be kind to yourself

    • Sometimes, it is our nature to compare ourselves to others, but we need to set a limitation on how far we compare both. Focusing on what you “can't” do might rob happiness away from you.

Recognizing Mental Health Concerns

Emotions, such as feeling sad or stressed is completely normal as a human. However, it is important to notice any emotional or behavioral changes that persist, become more severe, or even interfere with daily activities, as it may indicate that you need support.
Possible warning sign may include:

  • Persistent sadness/hopelessness/emptiness
  • Losing interest in activities that once were super fun and enjoyable for you
  • Ongoing feelings of anxiety
  • Changes in sleep or appetite
  • Withdrawing from friends and family
  • Difficult to concentrate or manage everyday responsibilities
  • Increased irritability
  • Unexplained changes in behavior

How to Cope with Retirement, Loss, and Other Life Changes?

Retirement, the death of a loved one, declining health, or moving into a new home can bring mixed emotions. These experiences may lead to sadness, loneliness and loss of purpose. Everyone adjusts at a different pace and there is no single correct way to respond.

  1. Create a new sense of purpose
  2. Allow yourself to grieve
  3. Familiar routines
  4. Talk about your feelings

How to Stay Connected and Overcome Loneliness?

Loneliness is the distressing feeling of lacking the connection you really want, while social isolation refers to having limited social contact. A person can feel lonely even when surrounded by others, and, conversely, living alone doesn't necessarily mean feeling lonely.
Social connections are very, very important for emotional well-being and overall health. Retirement. Bereavement. Mobility difficulties. Hearing problem. Changes in family circumstances. All of these can make staying connected more challenging.
Here are some ways to build and maintain connections.

  1. Schedule regular contact. Arrange phone calls/video calls/visits with friends and family.
  2. Join community activities. You can consider neighborhood gatherings, hobby groups, exercise classes, religious or cultural communities, senior activities, and many others.
  3. Reconnect with old friends
  4. Offer help when you can
  5. Address barriers to connection. If hearing, mobility, transportation, or digital access makes the communication difficult, ask family members or local community services about available support.

When and Where to Seek Help

Seeking help for mental health concerns is a positive step, not a sign of weakness-!!
You don't need to wait until symptoms become severe before talking about it to someone.

Where can you get support?

Start with a healthcare professional

In Indonesia, you can visit a nearby Puskesmas or a hospital to discuss your concerns and ask about available mental health services.

Ask about mental health professionals

Depending on your needs, a doctor may refer you to a qualified professional.

Reach out to trusted people

Family members. Friends. Caregivers. Community support groups. They can provide emotional support and help you access care.

Prepare for your appointment

Write down your symptoms, when they began, what are the changes in sleep or appetite, medicines you take, and questions you want to ask. If you feel comfortable, bring someone you trust.

Fitness & Wellness

How Can Older Adults Stay Active Every Day?

According to the National Health Service (NHS), older adults should be physically active every day even if it's just doing light activities. This is because regular physical activity can help you maintain the muscle strength in you in everyday life. Imagine how it would be without regular movements. Muscles can gradually become weaker which makes simple activities harder to do.
Staying active doesn't mean you have to go to the gym everyday or follow a strict exercise routine. There are so many simple ways to add movement to your daily life.

  1. Take a walk

    2. Stroll around your neighborhood, your garden, or any preferred, safe area at a comfortable pace.

  2. Do household chores

    3. You can choose to do gardening, sweeping, washing clothes, or light cleaning since they help to keep you moving.

  3. Stretch regularly

    4. Gentle stretching helps you maintain flexibility. 🤸‍♂️

  4. Take breaks from sitting

    5. You can stand up and move around instead of sitting for long periods.

  5. Enjoy activities with others

    6. Join a walking group or exercise with your friend, perhaps? It's a great way to stay motivated.

    Start your journey with activities which feel manageable. After your fitness improves, you may gradually increase the activities.

What Are the Benefits of Regular Physical Activity?

Regular physical activity supports physical and mental well-being. Even small amounts of movement can serve as a good starting point for people not used to exercising.
Some important benefits include:

  • Improve strength & muscle mass
  • Support bone density
  • Better balance & coordination which reduces fall risk which is a common cause of injury in older adults
  • Enhance endurance & stamina
  • Improved heart health
  • Greater independence
  • Better mood
  • Reduce stress & anxiety
  • Better sleep quality
  • Mobility maintained

Which Exercises Are Best for Older Adults?

The best exercises are those which match your physical abilities and health conditions. A balanced routine can include aerobic, strength, balance, and flexibility exercises.

Walking and Other Aerobic Activities

  • Walking
  • Cycling on a stationary bike

These exercises can improve endurance and heart health. Start at a comfortable pace. Remember to take breaks when needed too.

Strength Exercises

This type of exercise can aid in increasing muscle mass and also bone density. Examples include:

  • Chair sit-to-stands: practice standing up from a chair and sitting down slowly.
  • Wall push-ups: place your hands against a wall then gently bend and straighten your elbows
  • Cals raises: hold a sturdy chair or countertop and slowly raise your heels before lowering them.

Begin with a small number of repetitions. Focus on proper technique rather than speed.

Balance Exercises

Balance exercises help you practice to stay steady while you're moving around. One of the balance exercise examples is the single-leg stance.
You can also do pilates or chair yoga. In fact, chair yoga is highly recommended for seniors. It provides a safe and also low-impact way to exercise by using chair as a prop. When exercising, always choose a safe area. Use support when needed. If you have any significant balance problems, please do ask a healthcare professional's guidance before you try any sort of balance exercises on your own.

Flexibility Exercises

Gentle stretches for the shoulders, arms, legs, and ankles can help maintain your range of motion. Move slowly and avoid bouncing or forcing a stretch too.

How to Exercise Safely?

  • Start slowly, especially if it has been a while since the last time you exercised
  • Choose stable footwear
  • Find a non-slippery space
  • Use a sturdy chair or countertop for support if it's necessary
  • Drink enough water
  • Go rest whenever you need to
  • Avoid movements causing pain, dizziness, or unusual discomfort
  • Talk to a healthcare professional before you start a new exercise routine if you have a chronic health condition or recent injury/surgery.

Exercise Plan

A workout plan can help you build a regular exercise habit without feeling too overwhelmed!

Nutrition

If there are things that are important at every stage of life, one of them is eating well. Our bodies change over time, and, we may not feel as hungry as we used to. Some people may also find it harder to chew certain types of foods. Eating a variety of healthy foods helps older adults stay strong and also keep up with their daily activities. The good news is: healthy eating doesn't have to be super expensive.

How Do Nutritional Needs Change as You Age?

Over time as we age, our bodies may need different amounts of certain nutrients. Even if we eat way less than we used to, it's still important to get enough nutrients.

  • Protein for strong muscles. Our muscles can become weaker as we age - which is why we need to eat enough protein like eggs, fish, chicken, tofu, or tempe.
  • Calcium and vitamin D for healthy bones. These nutrients will help in keeping the bones strong. Therefore, reducing the risk of bone problems. Milk, yogurt and certain types of fish can help provide these to your body.
  • High-fiber foods. Constipation may become more common as we get older. To keep the digestion regular, you can eat vegetables, fruits, beans, and whole grains.
  • Vitamin B12 for the brain and nerves. Some older adults have trouble absorbing this vitamin. Vitamin B12 can be found in foods such as fish, meat, eggs, and dairy products, fortified breakfast cereal too.
  • Drink enough water throughout the day. Older adults usually don't feel thirst as often as younger people. The reason for this phenomenon is because our bodies undergo various changes as we get older. And one of them is hypothalamus which helps regulate thirst sensation, becoming less sensitive as time passes by. Therefore, even when our body needs more water, we might not feel thirsty at all.

What Should a Healthy, Balanced Diet for Seniors Include?

A healthy diet doesn't mean giving up all of your favorite foods. It's about eating foods that give your body the nutrients it needs

  1. Vegetables and fruits

    2. Add a little color to your plate-! 🎨 Leafy greens, carrots, papaya, bananas, and oranges. They provide vitamins and minerals.

  2. Protein-rich foods

    3. Adequate protein intake helps support muscle strength as you age. You can get it from everyday foods like lean meat, eggs, fish, chicken, tofu, poultry, cheese, cooked beans, or nuts and seeds.

  3. Carbohydrates and whole grains

    4. Your body would still need energy for daily activities. Whether you're taking a walk or doing household chores. Rice, oats, potato, and whole-wheat bread provide that energy. Those are some options to include in your meals. Whole grains are especially helpful because they contain more fiber.

  4. Calcium-rich foods

    5. Keeping your bones strong is very important. Milk and yogurt, along with calcium-fortified alternatives can help you get the calcium you need.

  5. Healthy fats

    6. Not all fats are bad for you. Fish, nuts, and vegetable oils provide fats that the body needs.

  6. Water and other healthy drinks

    7. Most importantly, remember to drink water regularly. Don't wait until you feel that thirst in your throat to have a drink. Older adults may not feel thirst as often, so you should try to drink water throughout the day. Unsweetened drinks are better for your health in this case.
    Here are a few simple ways to eat better every day:

    1. Try to eat meals at regular times, even when your appetite is smaller
    2. Make protein part of your main meals
    3. Choose fresh or minimally processed foods when you can
    4. If you have trouble chewing, try softer foods (e.g., porridge, steamed fish, soft vegetables, soup)
    5. Pick affordable but healthy ingredients

What Foods Should Older Adults Limit or Be Careful With?

Some foods are fine to enjoy occasionally. Eating too much of certain foods, however, may affect your health. Older adults should pay attention to food safety and any dietary advice related to their health conditions.

  • Foods high in salt: Instant noodles and processed meat, for example, contain a lot of salt. According to American Senior Communities, eating too much of these can raise blood pressure and strain the heart and kidneys. So try to choose other less salty options.
  • High in sugar foods and drinks: Lessen the consumption of sweetened drinks, candies, and desserts. They're absolutely fine to enjoy occasionally. But too much of sugar can make blood sugar hard to manage…
  • Raw/undercooked foods: Undercooked meat, undercooked seafood, or even undercooked eggs can carry harmful germs. Always remember to:
    1. Cook food thoroughly
    2. Wash your fruits and vegetables
    3. Store food properly
    These are very simple things, but it may reduce the risk of food poisoning.
  • Foods that are high in unhealthy fats: If you eat too much fried food, fatty processed meats, and certain baked goods, you may increase the risk of heart problems. Try eating foods prepared with healthier oil more often instead.
  • Alcohol: It'll increase the risk of falls. It also might interact dangerously with certain medicines.
  • Hard foods that are difficult to chew: It's better to eat softer foods when you have dental problems.

An important thing you should probably pay attention to is that your dietary needs can be different if you're a person who has a condition (e.g., diabetes, high blood pressure, kidney disease). You should ask a healthcare professional for advice before you make any major decisions concerning your diet or supplements.

Health Essentials

We might just feel healthy and not notice any significant problems during our daily lives, but some health conditions develop without any obvious symptoms. Hence, regular checkups (health screenings) and paying high attention to changes in your body would most likely help you spot health problems early on so that you are able to get the care you need.

Which Health Screenings Should You Get as You Age?

Here are some common health checks you'd want to discuss with your healthcare provider.

  1. Blood pressure check

    2. High blood pressure oftentimes had no obvious symptoms. By checking it regularly, you can detect early signs of heart disease and stroke earlier so you can take preventive actions.

  2. Blood sugar test

    3. This can help detect diabetes or prediabetes, particularly if you have risk factors. You should be careful if you have a family history of diabetes.

  3. Cholesterol test

    4. High cholesterol possibly increases the risk of heart disease. Be sure to heed warnings about high cholesterol levels along with its potential health risk.

  4. Bone health assessment

    5. As you age, your bones may become weaker which increases the risk of osteoporosis and fractures.

  5. Cancer screenings

    6. Depending on your age, sex, medical history, and risk factors, your healthcare provider may recommend screenings for certain cancers. For instance : colorectal or breast cancer, and cervical cancer when appropriate.

  6. Eye and hearing check

    7. Changes in eyesight and hearing can happen gradually. Through regular checks, you are able to identify problems which affect your daily activities and safety.

  7. Dental checkups

    8. Always keep your teeth and gums healthy so that it'll be easier for you to eat comfortably.

Not everyone needs the same exact tests or needs them at the same age. Deciding which screenings are appropriate, would be based on your health history and personal risk factors.

What Symptoms Should You Never Ignore?

It is normal to experience some changes as you age, but not every symptom should be dismissed as a normal part of getting older.
Below are some warning signs to look out for.

  1. Chest pain or difficulty in breathing

    2. These can be signs of serious health problems especially if they appear abruptly or are severe.

  2. Sudden weakness or trouble speaking

    3. Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, a drooping face, difficulty speaking. These may be signs of a stroke.

  3. Unexplained weight loss

    4. Losing weight without trying for a long time might indicate an underlying health issue.

  4. Persistent pain or unusual fatigue

    5. Pain which doesn't go away or tiredness that makes everyday tasks difficult to do should be checked by a healthcare professional.

  5. Changes in memory and thinking

    6. You should heed to confusion or memory problems you're experiencing. Beware if it starts to interfere your daily life.

  6. Changes in vision or hearing

    7. New/worsening difficulties can impact your independence and increase the risk of falls.

Don't wait for a routine checkup if something feels seriously wrong. Sudden chest pain. Breathing difficulties. Possible stroke symptoms require immediate emergency medical attention.

Are You Keeping Up With Your Regular Health Checkups?

It's easy to put off a health checkup when you feel fine or when you have a busy schedule. Regardless, regular checkups are still important. That is, even when you don't have any noticeable symptoms.
Here are a few ways to stay on top of your health:

  • Schedule regular checkups

    • Try to have your health checked thoroughly at least once a year or as often as your healthcare provider recommends.

  • Keep track of your own health

    • For example : write down your blood pressure, blood sugar results, medications, and any new symptoms so you can discuss them during your appointment.

  • Bring up any concerns

    • Even if something seems minor, let your healthcare provider know if you've noticed changes in the way your body behaves.

  • Don't skip follow-up visits

    • Follow your healthcare provider's advice about further tests/treatment if a health check reveals a problem.

Always keep in mind that taking care of your health doesn't start only when you feel sick. Making regular checkups a part of your routine can help you to stay informed, address health concerns earlier, and maintain your independence.
