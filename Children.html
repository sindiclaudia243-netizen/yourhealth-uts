Children's Health


Childhood is a period of rapid growth, learning, and discovery. Good health helps children feel secure, build relationships, explore the world, and reach their potential. Supporting children's wellbeing means caring for more than physical health: emotional support, nutritious food, active play, preventive care, and healthy development all matter. Explore practical guidance to help children grow in a safe, supportive, and healthy environment.

Mental Health

Children's mental health affects how they think, feel, learn, behave, and connect with other people. It develops through a combination of a child's experiences, relationships, environment, and individual characteristics. All children experience difficult emotions from time to time. However, ongoing emotional or behavioural difficulties can affect their learning, friendships, family relationships, and everyday activities. A safe and supportive environment helps children feel valued and better able to cope with challenges.

Recognize the Signs

Mental health difficulties do not always look the same in every child. Changes in behaviour may be a sign that a child needs extra attention or support. Possible signs include:

  • Frequent sadness, worry, fear, or unusual irritability.
  • Becoming withdrawn or losing interest in activities they usually enjoy.
  • Changes in sleeping patterns, appetite, or energy levels.
  • Repeated physical complaints, such as stomach aches or headaches, especially when stress may be involved.
  • Difficulty concentrating, learning, or participating at school.
  • Sudden changes in behaviour, friendships, or relationships with family members.

How Can Adults Support Children's Mental Health?

  • Listen and validate feelings. Give children time to explain what they feel. Avoid making them feel ashamed for being scared, sad, or angry.
  • Create a safe routine. Predictable daily routines can help children feel secure, especially during periods of change.
  • Encourage connection. Make time for conversation, play, family activities, and positive friendships.
  • Teach healthy ways to cope. Help children practise calming activities, problem-solving, and asking a trusted adult for help.
  • Work with the school. When difficulties affect learning or friendships, communication between caregivers and teachers can help identify useful support.

Remember: Seek advice from a qualified health or mental health professional when emotional or behavioural changes persist, become severe, or interfere with a child's daily life. If a child may be in immediate danger, seek urgent local help.

Fitness & Wellness

Physical activity is an important part of a healthy childhood. Moving regularly helps children develop stronger muscles and bones, improve coordination, support heart health, and build physical confidence. Active play can also support mood, sleep, social skills, and learning. Exercise should be enjoyable and suitable for a child's age, interests, abilities, and health condition. It does not need to involve competitive sports or expensive equipment.

Make Movement Part of Everyday Life

Children can be active in many different ways. A mixture of movement and play helps them practise different physical skills.

  • Active play: Running, jumping, dancing, skipping, and playground games help children develop coordination and movement skills.
  • Outdoor activities: Walking, cycling, swimming, and playing in a safe outdoor space encourage exploration and physical fitness.
  • Muscle- and bone-strengthening activities: Age-appropriate climbing, jumping, and active games help develop strength and healthy bones.
  • Social activities: Team games and group activities can help children practise cooperation, communication, and taking turns.

Balance Activity with Rest

Rest is just as important as movement. Children need sufficient sleep to support growth, memory, learning, mood, and overall wellbeing. A consistent bedtime, a calm sleeping environment, and reduced screen use before bed can help establish healthy sleep habits. Sleep requirements differ by age, so caregivers should follow age-appropriate recommendations.

Keep it positive: Focus on fun, participation, and gradual progress rather than appearance, weight, or performance. Children should not be pushed through pain or exhaustion.

Nutrition

Good nutrition provides the energy and nutrients children need to grow, learn, play, and stay healthy. Children's nutritional needs change as they develop, and appetite can vary from day to day. Offering a variety of nutritious foods and creating a positive mealtime environment can help children build healthy eating habits. The aim is to provide balanced choices without making children feel guilty or afraid of particular foods.

Build a Balanced Eating Pattern

  • Offer fruits and vegetables. Include different colours, textures, and varieties. Fresh, frozen, or suitably prepared options can all contribute to a balanced diet.
  • Choose whole grains and other sources of fibre. Foods such as oats, brown rice, whole-wheat bread, beans, and vegetables support digestion and provide energy.
  • Include protein-rich foods. Eggs, fish, chicken, tofu, tempeh, beans, lentils, and other suitable foods help support growth and tissue development.
  • Provide calcium-rich foods. Milk, yoghurt, cheese, or suitable fortified alternatives can contribute to healthy bones and teeth.
  • Encourage water. Make safe drinking water readily available, especially during hot weather and physical activity.
  • Limit frequent sugary drinks and highly processed snacks. Offer these less often while keeping everyday meals varied and satisfying.

Create Healthy Mealtime Habits

Children learn about food through repeated experiences. Eating together when possible, introducing unfamiliar foods patiently, and allowing children to respond to their hunger and fullness cues can support a healthier relationship with food. Avoid using food as a reward or punishment. Adults can guide food choices while giving children age-appropriate opportunities to participate, such as washing vegetables or choosing between two healthy snack options.

Important: Food needs differ according to age, growth, activity, allergies, medical conditions, and family circumstances. For concerns about growth, restricted eating, food allergies, or nutrient deficiencies, consult a qualified healthcare professional.

Health Essentials

Everyday preventive care helps protect children from illness and supports healthy development. Regular health visits give families an opportunity to discuss growth, nutrition, sleep, behaviour, vision, hearing, and other concerns. Children may not always be able to explain what is wrong, so caregivers play an important role in noticing changes and arranging care when needed.

Key Areas of Children's Health to Monitor

The care a child needs depends on their age, medical history, development, and individual circumstances. These are important areas to discuss with a healthcare professional.

  • Routine health and growth checks. Regular visits can help monitor a child's growth, development, nutrition, and general health. Bring questions or concerns to each appointment.
  • Vaccinations. Follow the recommended immunisation schedule in your area. Vaccines help protect children and communities from preventable infectious diseases.
  • Dental health. Encourage brushing with age-appropriate fluoride toothpaste, support healthy eating habits, and arrange dental visits according to professional advice.
  • Vision and hearing. Pay attention to signs such as difficulty seeing the board, frequent squinting, delayed responses to sounds, or difficulty following conversations.
  • Hygiene and infection prevention. Teach regular handwashing, safe food practices, and covering coughs and sneezes. Keep children home when they are too unwell to participate in normal activities.
  • Safety and injury prevention. Use appropriate car seats or seat belts, supervise water activities, store medicines and chemicals safely, and make play areas suitable for the child's age.

When Should a Child See a Healthcare Professional?

Arrange medical advice when symptoms are persistent, worsening, unusual for the child, or interfering with eating, drinking, sleeping, learning, or daily activities. Seek urgent medical care for severe breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, serious injury, or other emergency symptoms. When in doubt, contact a qualified healthcare professional rather than relying only on online information.

Be prepared: Keep a record of vaccinations, allergies, medicines, previous health concerns, and questions for the next appointment. This can help make health visits more useful.

Growth & Development

Children develop across several connected areas, including physical growth, movement, communication, thinking, and social-emotional skills. Each child develops at their own pace, although developmental milestones can help caregivers understand the skills many children demonstrate at different ages. Observing a child's progress over time is more useful than comparing them with other children.

Physical Growth and Growth Monitoring

Height, weight, and other growth measurements can help healthcare professionals understand how a child is growing over time. A single measurement does not tell the whole story; patterns across repeated measurements, together with the child's health and development, provide more useful information. Attend recommended growth checks and discuss concerns such as poor weight gain, unexpected weight changes, or difficulties with feeding.

Developmental Milestones

Milestones describe skills that many children develop in areas such as movement, communication, learning, and interaction. They are useful guides, not a test or a strict deadline. Caregivers can support development by talking, reading, playing, responding to a child's interests, and providing safe opportunities to explore.

Speech and Language Development

Communication develops through listening, sounds, gestures, words, and conversations. Talk with children during everyday activities, read books together, sing songs, and give them time to respond. If a child has difficulty understanding language, communicating needs, or making progress in speech, discuss this with a healthcare professional. Hearing assessment may also be important when communication concerns arise.

Social and Emotional Development

Children gradually learn to recognise feelings, manage frustration, share, cooperate, build friendships, and understand other people's perspectives. Adults can support these skills by modelling respectful communication, naming emotions, setting consistent boundaries, and helping children work through disagreements in an age-appropriate way.

How Can You Support Healthy Development?

  • Talk, read, sing, and play together every day.
  • Provide safe opportunities for children to explore and practise new skills.
  • Encourage curiosity and praise effort rather than demanding perfection.
  • Build predictable routines for meals, sleep, learning, and play.
  • Discuss developmental concerns early with a qualified healthcare professional.

Every child is different. If a child loses a skill they previously had, or you are concerned about their development, speak with a healthcare professional. Early discussion can help identify whether further assessment or support may be useful.
