WOMEN's HEALTH


Caring for your health means paying attention to both your physical and emotional needs. These needs may change during adolescence, pregnancy, after childbirth, and later in life. Understanding your body can help you build healthy habits and recognise when you need support. Start with manageable steps: eat varied meals, choose enjoyable physical activities, make time for rest, and discuss health concerns with a qualified healthcare professional.

Mental Health

Mental health affects how you think, feel, maintain relationships, and respond to everyday challenges. Women may experience emotional difficulties linked to personal circumstances, social pressures, caregiving responsibilities, or reproductive life changes. Some mental health conditions occur specifically around pregnancy, childbirth, or the menstrual cycle.

Everyday Ways to Support Your Mental Health

  • Stay Connected Make time to talk with trusted friends, family members, or other supportive people.
  • Manage your responsibilities. Set realistic goals, decide which tasks need attention first, and ask for help when needed.
  • Notice how social media affects you. Adjust your online habits if certain content leaves you feeling distressed or overwhelmed.
  • Care for yourself while supporting others. If you have caregiving responsibilities, discuss ways to share tasks and protect time for your own needs.

When to Seek Support

Speak with a healthcare professional if emotional difficulties persist, worsen, or interfere with studying, working, relationships, or daily care. Mental health conditions are treatable, and asking for support is a practical step toward recovery.

Fitness & Wellness

Physical activity is an important part of maintaining health throughout life. You do not need an expensive gym membership or a demanding workout programme to begin. Walking, dancing, cycling, and other accessible activities can help you become more active.

Building a Routine You Can Maintain

  • Start with your current ability. Choose an activity that feels manageable and gradually increase how much you do.
  • Choose something enjoyable. You are more likely to continue an activity that fits your interests and daily life.
  • Plan opportunities to move. Set aside realistic times for activity instead of waiting for a completely free day.
  • Find support. Invite a friend or family member to join you or encourage your progress.
  • Adapt as your needs change. Select activities suited to your age, abilities, and health circumstances.

Staying safe

Pay attention to how your body responds during and after activity. Discuss recurring dizziness, nausea, breathing difficulties, or pain with a healthcare professional. If a health condition affects your ability to exercise, ask for guidance on suitable activities.

Nutrition

Healthy eating means choosing a variety of foods that provide the energy and nutrients your body needs. Your overall eating pattern matters more than any single food, supplement, or special diet. Healthy meals can include familiar, affordable foods and do not have to follow complicated recipes.

Building Balanced Meals

  • Include vegetables and fruit. Aim to fill about half your plate with vegetables and fruit, and vary your choices.
  • Choose different protein sources. Include foods such as fish, eggs, lean meat, beans, tofu, tempeh, nuts, or seeds.
  • Include whole grains. Choose whole-grain options when available to add variety and fibre.
  • Include dairy or suitable alternatives. Choose options that help meet your nutritional needs.
  • Pay attention to added sugar, salt, and fats. Limit foods and drinks that contain large amounts of added sugar, sodium, saturated fat, or trans fat.

Making Changes Easier

Begin with one change you can maintain, such as adding vegetables to lunch, choosing fruit as a snack, or preparing meals at home more often. You can still enjoy favourite foods while considering how often and how much you eat.

Food needs vary with age and life stage. Use general meal-planning advice as a starting point and seek individual guidance when your health or circumstances require it.

Health Essentials

Preventive healthcare provides an opportunity to discuss your health, review potential risks, and decide which services are appropriate for you. A preventive visit can include conversations about physical health, emotional well-being, reproductive health, and everyday habits.

Preparing for a Health Visit

  • List your concerns. Write down symptoms, questions, and changes you have noticed.
  • Review your medical information. Bring details of medications, supplements, allergies, and previous health conditions.
  • Know your family history. Share relevant information about conditions that affect close relatives.
  • Discuss your priorities. Mention concerns about menstrual health, sexual health, pregnancy planning, or emotional well-being when relevant.
  • Clarify the next steps. Ask what any recommended test is for, how you will receive results, and whether follow-up is needed.

Understanding Screening

Screening tests look for certain health problems before symptoms appear. Depending on your circumstances, discussions may include breast and cervical cancer screening, alongside other preventive services.

Not every examination or test is needed at every visit. Appropriate care depends on your age, medical history, previous results, individual risks, and local guidelines. If you already have symptoms, tell your healthcare provider so they can assess the concern directly.

Period Education

Menstruation is a natural process in which blood and tissue from the lining of the uterus leave the body through the vagina. Understanding your menstrual cycle can help you prepare for your period, manage discomfort, and notice changes that may need medical attention.
Track when your period starts, how long it lasts, your flow, and any symptoms. Your usual pattern is useful information to share with a healthcare professional.

Nutrition Modifications During Menstruation

You do not need a special diet during menstruation. Continue eating varied, balanced meals that provide enough energy and nutrients. Paying attention to iron intake is particularly useful because menstrual bleeding causes iron loss.

Practical Steps

  • Include iron-rich foods such as lean meat, fish, beans, lentils, and iron-fortified cereals.
  • Pair plant-based iron sources with vitamin C foods, such as oranges, tomatoes, or bell peppers, to improve iron absorption.
  • Continue eating vegetables, fruit, whole grains, and protein foods.
  • Drink water regularly.
  • Ask a healthcare professional before taking iron supplements, especially if you suspect a deficiency.

Benefits

  • Helps meet daily energy and nutritional needs.
  • Supports adequate iron intake.
  • Pairing plant-based iron with vitamin C helps your body absorb it.

Possible Effects of Inadequate Nutrition

Insufficient iron intake, especially when combined with heavy menstrual bleeding, can contribute to iron-deficiency anemia. Symptoms may include tiredness, weakness, and shortness of breath. Food changes alone may not be enough to treat anemia or address heavy bleeding, so seek medical assessment if these symptoms occur.

Exercise and Activity During Menstruation

You can usually continue your normal activities during your period if you feel comfortable. Gentle movement may help ease menstrual cramps, but the type and intensity of activity should reflect how you feel.

Practical Steps

  • Try gentle walking, cycling, swimming, or yoga.
  • Reduce the intensity or length of your activity when symptoms make exercise uncomfortable.
  • Take breaks or rest when needed.
  • Stop exercising if you feel dizzy, unwell, or experience significant pain.
  • Seek advice if symptoms repeatedly prevent you from participating in normal activities.

Benefits

  • Gentle activity may help relieve period pain.
  • Adjusting your routine allows you to remain active within your comfort level.
  • Learning how symptoms affect activity can help you plan your routine.

Possible Effects of Pushing Through Symptoms

Continuing strenuous activity despite significant pain or dizziness may make it harder to exercise safely and comfortably. Resting when needed is appropriate. Severe or recurring symptoms should be assessed rather than repeatedly ignored.

Menstrual Symptoms and Self-Care

Cramps around the start of a period are common and may spread to the lower back or thighs. However, pain that disrupts daily activities or becomes worse deserves medical attention.

Practical Steps

  • Try a warm bath or shower.
  • Place a heat pad or hot water bottle wrapped in a towel on your abdomen.
  • Gently massage your abdomen or back.
  • Try gentle movement if it feels comfortable.
  • Record your pain, bleeding, and their effect on daily activities.
  • Ask a pharmacist or healthcare professional about suitable pain relief if needed.

Benefits

  • Heat, massage, and gentle activity may ease discomfort.
  • Tracking symptoms helps you describe changes during a consultation.
  • Medical assessment can help identify and treat underlying causes of persistent pain.

When to Seek Care

Seek prompt medical attention for severe or worsening pelvic pain, particularly if pain relief has not helped. Arrange a consultation if your periods become heavier, more painful, or irregular, or if you bleed between periods.

Bleeding lasting longer than seven days, needing to change a pad or tampon every one to two hours, or frequent tiredness and shortness of breath are also reasons to discuss your periods with a healthcare professional.

Possible Effects of Delaying Assessment

Persistent symptoms can interfere with studying, working, sleep, and everyday activities. Delaying assessment may also postpone treatment for an underlying condition or iron-deficiency anemia associated with heavy bleeding.

Menstrual Hygiene

Menstrual hygiene involves using products safely, keeping the external genital area clean, and having access to suitable washing and disposal facilities. Choose products that suit your comfort, flow, and circumstances.

Practical Steps

  • Wash your hands before and after changing menstrual products.
  • Change pads every few hours, or more frequently when your flow is heavy.
  • Change tampons every four to eight hours. Never leave one in for more than eight hours, and use the lowest absorbency needed.
  • Follow the manufacturer’s instructions for emptying, cleaning, and storing menstrual cups and other reusable products.
  • Wash reusable period underwear according to its care instructions.
  • Gently rinse the external genital area, or vulva, with water. Avoid washing inside the vagina, douching, or using scented genital products.
  • Wrap used disposable products and place them in a waste bin instead of flushing them.

Benefits

  • Helps maintain comfort and reduce irritation.
  • Regular product changes help prevent prolonged moisture buildup.
  • Following product instructions supports safer use.
  • Appropriate disposal helps keep toilets and shared facilities clean.

Possible Effects of Unsafe Product Use

Leaving products unchanged for too long can contribute to irritation, rashes, or infection. Scented products and internal cleansing may irritate the genital area or disturb its natural balance.

Seek medical advice if you experience unusual pain, a change in vaginal odor, or symptoms that are more severe than usual.
